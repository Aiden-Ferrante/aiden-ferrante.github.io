I read a lot about AI and I build a lot with it, and most of the commentary I run into is
someone restating an announcement with more confidence than the announcement had. I want to
write the other kind. Fewer opinions, and each one attached to something I actually went and
checked.

There are four things I keep coming back to, so those are the four things this site is about.

The first is the money and the hardware underneath all of it. People talk about AI like it is
software and it is much closer to heavy industry. Fabs, substations, water, power contracts
signed years before there is any revenue to justify them. I run my own financial data lake so
I can go query something instead of remembering a headline about it, and that is mostly what I
want to do here.

Second is robotics and what it does to work. I have a small robot on my desk running entirely
on my own hardware and it fails at simple things constantly. That is not industrial robotics,
but it is a lot more contact with the failure modes than most writing on this has.

Third is what these systems can actually do and how we would know, which is the one I care
about most. Every claim about AGI or about a model solving some domain comes out of a
measurement, and the measurements are in worse shape than people assume. One audit went
through ten agentic benchmarks and found task or reward problems in all ten. A do-nothing
agent passes a good chunk of one of the popular ones. I would rather read a capability claim
the way you read a study, and ask what was measured, under what budget, against what baseline.

Fourth is the race itself. The labs, open weights versus closed, and whatever regulation ends
up actually binding. I run almost everything on open models on my own hardware because I want
to, so when a good one ships with open weights my tools get better that week, and when one
does not I notice it directly.

I am not claiming authority on any of this. What I have is that I build the things I write
about, so I have somewhere specific to be wrong.

The rule I want to hold myself to is that before I publish something I should be able to say
what would change my mind and what I checked. When I cannot do that, I will say so instead.
