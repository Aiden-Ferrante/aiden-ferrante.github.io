There is no shortage of writing about artificial intelligence. There is a shortage of writing
about it by people who have to live with the consequences of being wrong.

Most of what circulates is a summary of someone else's announcement, reframed confidently. It
costs nothing to produce and nothing to be mistaken about, which is precisely why so much of it
exists. I would rather write the other kind: fewer claims, each one attached to something I
actually checked.

I have four standing interests. **The intelligence economy** — the compute, capital and energy
underneath an industry that talks about itself as though it were software. **Robotics and human
labor** — what automation can genuinely reach, which is narrower and stranger than the forecasts
assume. **Capability and the frontier** — what these systems can do and how we would know, given
that the benchmarks everyone quotes are in worse shape than the discourse admits. And **the
race** — the labs, open versus closed weights, and the regulation forming around them.

What I can offer on each is not authority. It is that I build the systems I am describing, so I
have somewhere specific to be wrong. When I claim something about running models locally, it is
because I do it. When I say a benchmark number is doing less work than it appears to, it is
because I went and read how the score was produced.

The standing rule for anything I publish here: I should be able to say what would change my
mind, and what I checked before writing. Where I cannot, I will say that instead of pretending
otherwise.
